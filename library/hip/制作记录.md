### 图是怎么生成的

1. `figures/tools/project-outlines.py` 把 Z-Anatomy 模型（与肩部 3D 同一份 `z-anatomy-1.4.0` 模型）里的骨和肌肉网格投影成前、后、外、内四个方向的二维轮廓，写到 `figures/tools/outlines.json`。
2. `figures/tools/hipfig.py` 是画图工具：按视角摆放骨与肌肉轮廓、画起止点、贴中英标签并自动避让。它建在共用的 `daily/engine/figlib/fig.py` 之上。
3. `figures/build-figures.py` 登记每张图（运动图在 `tools/figs_movement.py`，神经图在 `tools/figs_nerves.py`），并写出 `figure-labels.json`。
4. 重画：`python3 library/hip/figures/build-figures.py [图号…]`。

换到别的部位时，改投影脚本里的骨和肌肉名单、在 `build-figures.py` 里写新的图，工具本身不用改。

## 3. 需要 gui 知道的未核实项

- **穴位 WHO 英文定位**：GB29、BL54、LR11 三个穴位的 WHO 2008 原文没能打开，已从正文删去，只保留国标 GB/T 12346—2021 定位。其余 6 个穴位附 WHO 2008 原文。
- **穴位的层次、相关肌肉或触发点、部分“怎么找”**：根据一般局部解剖资料整理，不是穴位标准里的内容。
- **神经节段**：不同来源说法不一，统一写成“通常 …”，例如臀上神经 L4–S1、闭孔内肌神经 L5–S2、腘绳肌 L5–S2。
- **髋关节休息位**（约屈 30°、外展 30°、轻度外旋）取自常用 PT 教材（Magee），没有在打开的网页上确认。
- **单腿站立的数值**来自简化杠杆模型，不同模型差别大：外展肌力 1.6–2.5 倍体重，关节力 2.4–2.6 倍体重，部分模型约 4 倍。图 15 左侧模型图上的力臂长度只是示意，真实比例看右侧杠杆图。
- **梨状肌在屈髋约 60° 以上转为内旋**：阈值各家说法在 60–90° 之间，采用约 60°。
- **长收肌约 70° 屈髋时由屈转伸**：出自 WikiMSK，该页没有列参考文献，属于近似值。
- **步态肌电时间窗（图 17）**：由分期表换算成大致的百分比，不是某一篇研究的原始数据。
- PubMed、PMC 页面多次出现验证码，Europe PMC 限流，所以 Neumann 2010 和 Delp 1999 没有引用。

## 附录：资料核对笔记（英文原文）

### 臀部与深层外旋肌（m1）

- Superior gluteal nerve roots: Kenhub gives L4–S1, StatPearls gives L4, L5 and S1; recorded as usually L4–S1.
- Nerve to obturator internus (obturator internus and superior gemellus): Kenhub gives L5–S1, StatPearls gemelli article gives L4–S2; recorded as usually L5–S2 (common textbook value); not uniformly agreed.
- Piriformis innervation: StatPearls says usually S1–S2 anterior rami, sometimes S2 only or L5–S2.
- Piriformis internal rotation above about 60 degrees of hip flexion is supported by LearnMuscles (Muscolino); StatPearls, Kenhub and Physiopedia only state lateral rotation in extension and abduction in flexion. The exact threshold varies in the literature (about 60–90 degrees); 60 degrees is used.
- Gluteus medius posterior fibres external rotation: StatPearls says 'when the knee is in extension' (likely meaning the hip/limb extended); recorded simply as 'assist external rotation'.
- Tensor fasciae latae: Kenhub's gluteal overview says it externally rotates the leg (knee), while its dedicated page lists hip flexion, abduction and internal rotation; hip actions used. The insertion on Gerdy's tubercle is standard but was named on the pages only as the lateral condyle of the tibia.
- Obturator externus innervation via the posterior branch of the obturator nerve is the standard textbook description; Kenhub as opened stated only obturator nerve L3–L4.
- Obturator internus origin: Kenhub's table and text disagree on the obturator membrane surface wording; the standard inner (pelvic) surface is used.

### 前群、内收肌群、腘绳肌（m2）

- Hamstring spinal segments differ by source: Kenhub gives L5–S2 for biceps femoris, semitendinosus and semimembranosus, while TeachMeAnatomy only states the sciatic nerve as a whole (L4–S3). Records use 'usually L5–S2'. Many texts (e.g. Moore) give L5–S2 or S1–S2 for the short head.
- Adductor magnus hamstring part: Kenhub gives L4 for the tibial-division supply; TeachMeAnatomy gives L4–S3 (the whole sciatic nerve). Record uses 'usually L4'.
- Pectineus: Kenhub gives the femoral nerve as L2–L3, and its obturator/accessory obturator contribution inconsistently (L2–L3 in its table, L3–L4 in its text). TeachMeAnatomy says only 'possible branch from the obturator nerve'. The record does not give segments for the obturator contribution.
- Pectineus is classed with the anterior compartment by TeachMeAnatomy but with the medial (adductor) group by Kenhub and most texts; it is coloured as medial group (blue).
- Gracilis segments: Kenhub L2–L3; TeachMeAnatomy L2–L4 (as for the obturator nerve generally). Record uses 'usually L2–L3'.
- Sartorius segments: Kenhub L2–L3; TeachMeAnatomy gives only the femoral nerve generally (L2–L4).
- Psoas major origin: Kenhub's text gives T12–L4 vertebral bodies plus lumbar transverse processes (its table lists L1–L5 transverse processes). Hip rotation by psoas/iliopsoas and adductor rotation actions are debated and were left out of the actions.
- Rectus femoris rows rely on Elsevier and TeachMeAnatomy; the Kenhub rectus femoris page could not be fetched. NCBI StatPearls pages were blocked by a CAPTCHA during research and are not cited.
- Semimembranosus 'flat membranous tendon' proximal description is standard textbook wording (origin of the name), not explicitly stated on the fetched Kenhub page.

### 骨、关节、神经（n1）

- Hip joint close-packed position: Physiopedia gives full extension only; many PT texts (e.g. Magee) give extension + medial rotation + abduction. Written as 'usually' extension with medial rotation and abduction.
- Hip loose-packed (resting) position (about 30° flexion, 30° abduction, slight lateral rotation) is from standard PT texts (Magee) and was not confirmed on an opened web page (G-03). Physiopedia instead calls flexion + abduction + lateral rotation the position of optimal articular contact.
- Hip capsular pattern: Cyriax table lists flexion, abduction, medial rotation with order varying by stage; other texts put medial rotation first. Written as 'usually' with a variation caveat.
- Spiral arrangement of capsular ligaments tightening in extension: TeachMe confirms the spiral course of the ischiofemoral ligament and Physiopedia that the capsule is tight in extension; the explicit 'all three wind spirally' wording comes from standard texts (G-03).
- Pubofemoral origin from the obturator crest, ischiofemoral being the weakest, and the ligament of the head of femur being slack in most positions are standard textbook statements not explicitly on the opened pages (G-03).
- Femoral neck anteversion: Kenhub gives 10–15°; other sources give about 8–15°. Neck-shaft angle about 125–130° depending on source.
- Artery of ligamentum teres: Wheeless says it arises from the obturator artery or the medial circumflex femoral artery; usually described as from the posterior branch of the obturator artery.
- Sciatic nerve–piriformis variants: meta-analysis (45 studies, 7,068 limbs) gives type A 85.2%, type B 9.8%; TeachMe says divisions separate at exit in about 12%. Rarer types (common fibular above piriformis, whole nerve through piriformis) are below about 2% each; exact rare-type percentages not verified (G-03).
- Sciatic nerve division level: usually at the apex of the popliteal fossa, but it can occur anywhere from the pelvis down; stated as 'usually'.
- Common fibular division being more vulnerable (foot drop) after hip dislocation/arthroplasty is standard clinical teaching but was not confirmed on an opened page (G-03).
- Obturator nerve supply to adductor magnus: Kenhub is internally inconsistent; used the majority view (adductor part only, ischial part by tibial division).
- Pectineus: usually femoral nerve, sometimes also obturator (or accessory obturator).
- Nerve to quadratus femoris and nerve to obturator internus injury/entrapment statements are inferred from their course (deep gluteal space, ischial spine); no dedicated clinical source opened (G-03).
- Lumbar plexus injury during lateral transpsoas spinal surgery and lumbosacral trunk compression in childbirth are standard clinical teaching, not confirmed on an opened page (G-03).
- Greater sciatic foramen boundaries: TeachMe adds the anterior sacroiliac ligament superiorly; Kenhub mentions only the sacrotuberous and sacrospinous ligaments.
- Lateral femoral cutaneous nerve roots usually L2–L3; StatPearls notes L1–L2 or L3–L4 origins also occur, and the exit point is usually about 1 cm medial to ASIS but can be several centimetres medial.
- Gluteal surface of ilium as the origin of the gluteal muscles, posterior sacroiliac ligaments at PSIS, obturator externus origin from the outer obturator membrane/margin, and the gluteal tuberosity receiving the deep fibres of gluteus maximus are standard textbook facts; the opened pages only said 'gluteus maximus attaches' (G-03).
- NCBI Bookshelf pages were intermittently behind a reCAPTCHA; the femoral nerve StatPearls chapter (NBK556065) was read successfully, other StatPearls chapters were read via statpearls.com.

### 穴位与临床（c1）

- GB/T 12346-2021 Chinese location text for all 9 points was read from the SHUTCM-hosted PDF and is quoted verbatim in location.zh. location.en is a translation of the GB/T text; WHO 2008 wording is kept separately in an extra field 'locationWho' (not in the shoulder example shape; merge into location if preferred).
- WHO 2008 wording verified directly (medbox copy) for BL36, BL37, ST31, ST32. The medbox text copy ends at about BL46, so GB29, GB30, GB31, BL54 and LR11 could not be read there. GB30's WHO wording was confirmed from the JOSR 2022 acupoint table (opening matches) and GB31's from a trial table (PMC6347816) matching WHO phrasing. GB29, BL54 and LR11 WHO English wordings were written from knowledge and are NOT verified (G-03).
- GB30: WHO 2008 is generally reported to carry a Remarks/alternative location for GB30; this could not be verified and is not included.
- GB31 disagreement: GB/T 12346-2021 locates it 9 cun above the popliteal crease at the posterior border of the iliotibial band, whereas WHO 2008 uses the middle-fingertip method (standing, arms hanging). Both are given.
- Acupoint 'layers', 'target' (trigger-point/dry-needling) and parts of 'howToFind' are compiled from general regional anatomy sources (TeachMeAnatomy, Kenhub, Elsevier, Orthobullets) and are not from the acupoint standards; the standards do not describe layers. Specific trigger-point referral statements (e.g. TFL referral to lateral thigh) are standard teaching (Travell & Simons) but no Travell & Simons page was opened (G-03).
- ST30 location used in LR11 howToFind (level with upper border of pubic symphysis, 2 cun lateral, medial to femoral pulse) was not re-read from GB/T in this task.
- Inferior gluteal nerve root divisions: Kenhub says posterior divisions of anterior rami (L5–S2); Orthobullets says 'ventral divisions'. Posterior (dorsal) division is the usual textbook statement; record lists L5–S2 only.
- Superior gluteal nerve roots given as 'usually L4–S1' (Orthobullets, StatPearls).
- Gluteus maximus lurch described from Orthobullets (trunk leans backward at heel strike); Kenhub only says 'lurching gait'.
- Hip scour and log roll: sources state no sensitivity/specificity (log roll only inter-rater kappa 0.61, Martin & Sekiya 2008), so none given. Squeeze test: no accuracy numbers stated by opened sources. Thomas and Ober: only reliability data, no sensitivity/specificity.
- FABER accuracy figures come from Physiopedia's summary of a 2003 Clin J Sport Med study (sensitivity 0.89) and Troelsen 2009; FADIR from Caliesch 2020 via Physiopedia; FAIR from Fishman 2002 via Physiotutors; SLR from van der Windt (Cochrane) via PT Progress; Ely from Physiopedia. Primary papers were not opened.
- Hamstring strain: semimembranosus involvement in stretch-type injuries is standard teaching (Askling) but the opened Physiopedia summary did not state it explicitly (G-03).
- Obturator nerve: 'circumducting gait secondary to an externally rotated hip' is from GPnotebook; TeachMeAnatomy only says 'posture and gait problems'.
- Several NCBI/PMC pages returned a reCAPTCHA and physio-pedia Scour Test was rate-limited, so those were not used. Source ids use the c1- prefix; c1-gbt-12346-2021 and c1-who-2008-acupoint-locations duplicate the shoulder chapter's acupoints-gbt-12346-2021 and clinical-who-2008-acupoint-locations (same URLs) and can be remapped on merge.

### 运动与生物力学（mv1）

- Fetch failures (G-03): PubMed and PMC pages (pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov, pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov) returned a reCAPTCHA interstitial; Europe PMC was rate-limited (HTTP 429) by the fetch proxy; jospt.org and kenhub gluteus medius page failed to load. Neumann 2010 (JOSPT, Kinesiology of the hip: a focus on muscular actions) and Delp et al. 1999 (Variation of rotation moment arms with hip flexion) could therefore not be opened and are not cited.
- Piriformis reversal angle: Muscolino gives 'approximately 60 degrees or more'; other texts (not opened, e.g. Delp 1999 modelling, Neumann) are often quoted as 60–90°. Physiopedia and Kenhub only say it abducts the flexed hip; they do not state the reversal. Kept 'about 60° or more'.
- Adductor sagittal-plane switch: the ~70° flexion threshold for adductor longus comes from WikiMSK, which lists no references; treat as approximate. Neumann's textbook describes the same dual flexor/extensor role but was not opened. The sprinting example is a common teaching point not verified in an opened source.
- Adductor magnus moment-arm figures (extension ~67 mm vs adduction ~20 mm posterior portion) are from the J Appl Physiol paper; the 'about three times' wording is derived from those medians. Publication year not confirmed (DOI suffix 2024).
- Single-leg stance numbers are from simple lever models (Orthobullets: 12.5 cm vs 5 cm, abductor 2.5 × BW, JRF about 3.5–4 × BW; Harrison/Hobbie: abductor 1.6 × BW, JRF 2.4 × BW) and OUHSC (2.4–2.6 × BW stance, 4–7 × BW gait). Models disagree; the text gives ranges. Body weight acting on the stance hip excludes the stance limb (about 1/7 body weight in Harrison's model). Cane figures (0.6 × / 1.3 ×) assume the cane bears about 1/6 of body weight and is about 30 cm from the midline.
- Plane for flexion/extension (sagittal) and the medial-lateral and anteroposterior axes are standard terminology; Kenhub states coronal plane for abduction/adduction and horizontal plane about the mechanical axis for rotation, but does not state the sagittal plane explicitly.
- Force couples: no single opened source states all three couples in force-couple language. The anterior/posterior tilt muscles come from Wikipedia (Pelvic tilt) and Physiopedia (Lower Crossed Syndrome); the frontal-plane couple from Physiopedia Gluteus Medius (ipsilateral TFL and contralateral QL) and StatPearls Trendelenburg sign. 'Erector-spinae', 'quadratus-lumborum' and 'rectus-abdominis' ids are not among the 22 chapter muscles. The task suggested contralateral adductors as part of the frontal-plane couple; this was not found in an opened source and was omitted.
- Lumbopelvic rhythm: no opened source gave absolute degree contributions (e.g. the commonly quoted ~40° lumbar and ~70° hip for toe-touching); only qualitative early-lumbar / late-hip sequencing (Esola 1996, via Moenter summary) and the L/P ratio from Hasebe 2016 (12 healthy men, pelvic motion measured as sacral inclination with Spinal Mouse; phase I–IV values only in figures). Esola 1996 abstract itself was not readable; only bibliographic metadata.
- Gait windows are phase-based approximations: muscle-to-phase assignments from the Rancho Los Amigos table (Hall & Brody Table 20-3) and the OUHSC summary, mapped onto Perry phase percentages from AAPM&R (IC 0–2, LR 2–12, MSt 12–31, TSt 31–50, PSw 50–62, ISw 62–75, MSw 75–87, TSw 87–100). They are not exact EMG onset/offset values, which (Perry & Burnfield) could not be opened. Physiopedia gives slightly different boundaries (LR 0–10, MSt 10–30, PSw 50–60, ISw 60–75, MSw 75–85). Gluteus medius activity into terminal stance is per OUHSC only; Rancho lists abductors through midstance. Adductor magnus window uses the generic 'adductors' entries in terminal swing (Rancho) and loading response (OUHSC). Iliopsoas window 31–87 combines eccentric terminal stance (OUHSC) with iliacus in pre-swing to mid swing (Rancho/OUHSC). Sartorius external rotation was not listed in the opened action sources and so is not in the actionTable.
- Two-joint muscles: sources are a fitness-education article (NFPT) and a chiropractic CE course chapter; definitions are standard. The rectus femoris 'active insufficiency' example (upright seated knee extension) follows NFPT's statement about reduced tension, which is phrased loosely there.
